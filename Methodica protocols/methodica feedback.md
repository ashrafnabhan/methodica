# SI and Lactate

- Mismatch between rationale and target population: The protocol relies on evidence from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients to justify a study in polytrauma patients.
- No single clearly defined primary objective: Multiple primary aims are proposed (mortality prediction, ICU admission, predictor comparison, composite models, correlations, score development).
- Polytrauma definition: uses non-standard definitions (e.g., ≥2 body regions involved, ISS >10) instead of validated criteria such as the Berlin definition.
- Overly restrictive exclusion criteria:
  - Excluding elderly patients, severe head injury, diabetes, substance use, and other high-risk groups reduces generalizability and introduces selection bias.
  - Excluding patients who die early creates survival bias when mortality is the main outcome.
  - Exclusion of patients with missing data systematically removes the least-investigated patients.
- Restricting enrollment to patients with elevated SI and/or lactate excludes normal-value patients making assessment of predictive value impossible.
- Primary outcome must be clearly defined by a subject-area expert: in-hospital mortality, 24-hour mortality, 48-hour mortality, and 30-day mortality.
- Design inconsistency: query prospective, yet data collection is based on date extraction from hospital records.
- Use of inappropriate variables for trauma research: Cardiac arrest variables such as ROSC, bystander CPR, and shockable rhythm are not relevant to trauma populations.
- Major trauma confounders are omitted e.g., ISS, GCS, injury pattern, mechanism of injury, comorbidities, medications, and trauma interventions.
- Incorrect measurement units: SI and MSI are dimensionless indices, should not be reported using mmol/L.
- Reliance on retrospective hospital records increases risk of information bias and missing data.
- Objectives are not achievable with the proposed design e.g., estimating prevalence of polytrauma or shock frequency within a cohort restricted to polytrauma cases.
- Several anticipated findings appear pre-specified, creating concern that conclusions are assumed before data collection.

# PPD and MoD

- Study design is inconsistent with the research question: cross-sectional design to assess "risk".
- Design descriptions are internally inconsistent: cross-sectional design while incorporating repeated follow-up assessments over several months.
- MoD is inconsistently categorized. Some protocols combine all CS despite evidence that elective and emergency CS may have different associations with PPD.
- Selection bias: Recruitment from psychiatric clinics, postnatal clinics, or vaccination clinics may not yield participants representative of the wider postpartum population.
- Excluding women likely to be lost to _follow-up_ introduces additional selection bias.
- Use of EPDS-defined controls with exclusion of intermediate scores creates spectrum & selection bias.
- Overly restrictive eligibility criteria remove many real-world obstetric cases and limit external validity.
  - The striking example is restriction to uncomplicated primigravidae only.
- Flawed Matching in case-control studies. Matching controls on MoD (the exposure being investigated), can eliminate the very association under study.
- Inadequate confounders: e.g. marital conflict, intimate partner violence are incompletely addressed
- Internal contradiction: Previous depression is often listed as a confounder while women with prior psychiatric illness are excluded from the study.

# ADHD and MoD

- Cases and controls may not arise from the same source population, creating a major risk of selection bias.
- Insufficient assurance that controls represent the population that produced the cases.
- Recruitment from tertiary psychiatry centers may limit representativeness, since referred ADHD cases may differ from typical community ADHD cases.
- Exposure assessment relies on parental recall years after birth, creating risk of recall bias for perinatal factors.
- Excluding children with parental psychiatric illness may bias results, because parental mental health is itself strongly associated with ADHD risk
- Several protocols include very large numbers of confounders relative to projected sample size, increasing the likelihood of overfitting and unstable regression estimates.

# Others

- Mismatch between study design and research question:
  - Case-control studies to compare effectiveness of smoking cessation interventions.
  - Cross-sectional studies attempting to infer causal relationships or diagnostic delays.
- Objectives must be specific.
- Secondary aims frequently introduce distinct research questions, resulting in objective creep.
- Major internal inconsistencies in study design descriptions: XS, cohort, case-control, or genetic association research in different sections.
- Outcomes are often poorly defined, subjective, or insufficiently validated.
  - Diagnostic delay and appropriate treatment lack objective verification.
  - Smoking cessation relies primarily on self-report.
  - PPD is measured using screening instruments rather than confirmed diagnoses.
- Recall bias is a major threat across several protocols.
  - Query recall childbirth experiences, AI use, smoking history, physician consultations, or treatment timelines.
  - Individuals with depression may differentially recall or interpret past experiences.
- Important confounders are inadequately measured or omitted.
  - Pre-pregnancy and antenatal depression in PPD studies.
  - Motivation and nicotine dependence in smoking cessation studies.
  - Clinical and environmental risk factors in genetic studies.
- Confounding by indication is a major concern in comparative effectiveness studies, because individuals selecting different treatments differ systematically in motivation, disease severity, and baseline characteristics.
- Recruitment targets appear unrealistic, e.g., uncommon conditions such as early-onset Alzheimer's disease.
- Protocols frequently combine multiple complex objectives into a single study, including biomarker development, genetic prediction, phenotype characterization, treatment response assessment, and risk modeling.
- Age at diagnosis is incorrectly used as a proxy for age at disease onset.
- Enthusiasm 🡪 Multiple secondary genomic objectives 🡪 dilute the primary research question.
